Pick a color. Get a palette that actually works.

One base color in, a full design-ready scale out — with contrast checked against real text, so the palette you ship is the palette people can read. No sign-up, no build step, nothing leaves your browser. The same math runs in Node — require("./palettemint.js"), one file, no install.

Harmonies

How it looks to everyone else

Roughly 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women see color differently. These simulations (Machado et al. matrices) show your base color the way they would see it — check before you build a UI that depends on telling two colors apart.

And in code, the same toolkit

      
The playground above is a thin page over the library. Use it in Node
      or the browser — one file, zero dependencies.

      

        

          Parse anything
          
Hex (3, 4, 6, 8 digits), rgb(), hsl(), and all
          148 CSS named colors. Bad input throws a ColorError that
          tells you what went wrong.

        

        

          Real WCAG math
          
Relative luminance and contrast ratios per WCAG 2.1, with AA / AAA
          verdicts for normal and large text. Black on white is 21:1, as it
          should be.

        

        

          Palettes & harmonies
          
Tints, shades, Tailwind-style 50–950 scales, complementary /
          analogous / triadic / square harmonies, and color-blindness
          simulation. Deterministic colors from strings, too.
